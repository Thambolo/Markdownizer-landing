Workplace privacy

Is it safe to paste work documents into ChatGPT or another AI tool?

Do not start with whether you can log in. Start with whether you are allowed to share the document with that specific service and account.

Whether it is safe to paste a work document into ChatGPT or another AI tool depends first on your organization's rules, the document owner's permission, the document's sensitivity, and the destination service and account. Being able to sign in or access a page is not the same as being allowed to send its contents to an external service. If the policy is unclear, do not paste the material until you confirm an approved workflow.

Check permission and purpose first

Ask who owns the document, whether it contains client, employee, customer, financial, health, legal, security, or confidential information, and whether your role permits sharing it for this purpose. Check contracts, confidentiality terms, data-classification rules, and workplace AI policies. If an administrator or data owner must approve a service, get that approval before using it.

A work login, paid plan, private conversation, or private browser window does not by itself establish permission. Likewise, a tool's ability to open a webpage behind a login says nothing about whether the page can be copied or sent elsewhere. For access-versus-permission distinctions, see the login-required page guide.

Review the destination service and account

Check the exact product, account type, workspace settings, retention terms, administrator controls, and available data-use settings. Features and policies can differ across plans and may change. Do not assume that a personal account has the same protections as an organization-managed service or that a setting for model improvement controls every form of retention, access, or compliance.

For ChatGPT, OpenAI's Data Controls documentation says the available controls depend on whether you are signed in, the plan, and workspace settings; it also distinguishes model-training controls from other data controls. Use the current documentation and ask your workspace owner about applicable organization policies. This is product-specific guidance, not a conclusion about whether your employer permits a particular document to be shared.

Minimize what leaves your approved environment

  • Use the smallest excerpt that supports the task instead of uploading a whole folder.
  • Remove names, account IDs, confidential amounts, secrets, and unrelated records when policy permits redaction.
  • Check links and query parameters; a URL may contain an identifier or access-bearing value.
  • Keep code, tables, and caveats only when they are necessary, and review the result after conversion.
  • Do not paste passwords, API keys, session tokens, or other credentials into a prompt.

Redaction can fail if the remaining context still identifies a person or project, and careless edits can change meaning. Follow your organization's de-identification rules rather than inventing a personal method for sensitive data.

Understand each handoff

If a webpage conversion tool is part of your workflow, check its own policy too. Markdownizer tokenizes readable page text locally before sending a structural skeleton for conversion; that skeleton can still contain links, image references, and other HTML attributes. The authoritative Markdownizer privacy policy explains what is and is not stored in analytics. Copying or downloading the output and pasting it into an AI service is a separate sharing event with that service.

A stop-or-proceed checklist

  1. Can I share this content with this service for this purpose?
  2. Is this the approved account or workspace?
  3. Have I checked retention, access, and data-use settings with current sources?
  4. Can I remove unnecessary text and private link details without losing meaning?
  5. Have I reviewed the final prompt and recipient before sending?

If any answer is uncertain, ask your organization's privacy, security, legal, or data owner. For a webpage-specific content review, see what to review before sharing a webpage with AI.
