Workflow and privacy

Using webpages that require login as AI context

A URL points to a page. It does not necessarily give your AI tool access to what you see.

An AI tool opening a link may not have your browser's signed-in session. A course portal, internal documentation site, or workspace page can therefore be visible to you but unavailable to the tool. Its response may be incomplete because it never received the source material.

One option is to provide an approved excerpt directly. Markdownizer can help convert content from a webpage you already have open into Markdown for your prompt. This does not bypass access controls, and it does not mean you have permission to share everything you can view.

Access and permission are different questions

Before converting or pasting material, ask both:

  • Can I view this page? You must already have legitimate access to the content in your browser.
  • Can I share this content with this AI service? Your workplace, course, client, or the source's terms may restrict sharing, even when your login allows you to read it.

If the second answer is unclear, do not paste the material. Ask the relevant owner or use an approved internal workflow instead.

A careful browser-to-prompt workflow

  1. Open the page normally. Sign in through the site's usual process and load the specific content you need.
  2. Check sharing rules. Confirm that the destination AI tool is appropriate for the source.
  3. Convert the relevant material. Open Markdownizer and start the conversion. Review the result rather than assuming the whole workspace has been captured.
  4. Remove unnecessary details. Exclude personal data, account information, private links, and unrelated content.
  5. Paste the source alongside a clear task. State what the AI should do and what it should treat as reference material.
  6. Check the answer against the page. Verify requirements, numbers, and claims before relying on the response.

For a course page you are allowed to share, a focused question might be: "Use these assignment requirements to make an outline. Do not write the assignment. Mark which sections require sources." This keeps the task narrower than "Do this for me" and makes the expected output easier to check.

Review links as well as visible text

A page can contain sensitive information outside its paragraphs. Links may reveal private resource paths or include identifying query parameters. Image references and other attributes may also contain information you do not want to send to another service.

Check the converted output for those details before copying it. Share the minimum necessary context rather than a complete portal page simply because it is convenient.

What Markdownizer's privacy model does and does not mean

Markdownizer tokenizes readable page text locally in your browser. It sends a structural skeleton for conversion, then restores the readable Markdown in your browser. That structural payload can include links, image references, and other HTML attributes.

Conversion is therefore not entirely local. Markdownizer also collects limited operational data as described in its privacy policy. Read that policy for the authoritative details rather than assuming that a privacy-focused design means no data leaves the browser.

When you later paste Markdown into an AI service, you are making a separate sharing decision. That service's own terms, retention controls, and account configuration apply. Markdownizer does not control how the destination handles your pasted content.

Some pages still will not convert well

Being able to see a page does not guarantee that its content can be extracted into useful Markdown. Browser-restricted pages, embedded documents, complex layouts, or content that has not loaded can cause limitations. Visual information may need a different workflow.

Check the current compatibility and limitations before relying on a particular page type. If an essential section is missing or the result is ambiguous, use an authorized source export or a carefully reviewed excerpt instead.
