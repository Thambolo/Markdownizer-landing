Capture options

Smart selection vs. full-page capture: which should you use?

Start with the main content when it is enough; widen the scope only when the missing context matters.

Use Markdownizer's default Smart selection when the page has a clear article or main-content area and that is what you need. Choose Capture full page when important material is outside that area, such as a relevant note elsewhere in the page body. Then inspect the Markdown: Smart selection can fall back to the body, and full-page capture can include navigation or unrelated content.

Neither mode guarantees that every section on a dynamic page has loaded. The choice is about capture scope, not access, scrolling, or a rendered Markdown preview. The current feature guide lists the extraction behavior and limits.

What Smart selection does

Smart selection is the default. It looks for the page's article or main-content area, including elements marked as main content, and falls back to the body when it cannot find a suitable target. The intention is to focus on the central material without assuming every item around it belongs in your prompt.

That focus is useful for a news story, a long help article, or a product description where the page has a clear central region. But web pages are not all structured the same way. A site might place definitions, a changelog, citations, or an important warning outside the element identified as the main article. Extraction rules cannot know which sidebar or note matters to your particular question.

What full-page capture changes

When you enable Capture full page, Markdownizer captures the page body instead of using Smart selection for that conversion. This may bring in sections missed by a narrower selection. It can also bring in header links, recommendations, repeated navigation, and unrelated material, which makes the output longer and harder to inspect.

The setting is for the current conversion and resets to Smart selection when you reopen the popup. If you choose full-page capture, check the setting before starting rather than assuming it will carry over from a previous page.

Full-page does not mean every possible page section

A long page may load more content as you scroll, retrieve results after an interaction, or place content in a frame that the extension cannot read. Full-page capture does not automatically scroll through the page to trigger loading, bypass a login, or turn a PDF or browser-internal page into an ordinary webpage conversion. Eligible same-origin frames may be included, while cross-origin or inaccessible frames are skipped.

If a section is absent, first make sure it is actually visible and loaded in the browser. Then check the converted text. If the source itself is inaccessible or sharing is not permitted, do not try to work around that restriction.

Use a quick decision test

  • Choose Smart selection when the main article answers your question and you want less surrounding page material to review.
  • Try full-page capture when a relevant body section appears to be outside the main article area.
  • Use another authorized source when key information is inside an inaccessible frame, an unloaded interactive view, or a document type the extension does not support.

For example, if you need an article's methods and limitations, first check whether those sections are in the main content. If the page puts an important data note in a separate body section, full-page capture may include it. This is an example of choosing scope, not a guarantee that a particular website will be extracted in a specific way.

Review output, not the webpage overlay

The selection preview is a highlight on the webpage that indicates what is selected for capture. It is not a Markdown preview or an editor inside the popup. After conversion, the popup provides status, a character count, and output actions. Copy or download the Markdown, then inspect or edit it in another app.

Look for the material you asked for, the heading hierarchy, table labels, link destinations, and leftover navigation. If full-page capture creates clutter, trim it outside the extension and keep the source title and link.
