Research notes

How do I turn online research into organized notes?

Use a consistent record for the question, the source, the evidence, what remains uncertain, and what you will check next.

Turn online research into organized notes by creating a separate record for each source, preserving its identity, and writing down the claim, supporting evidence, limits, and follow-up questions in a consistent format. Organize notes around your research question, but keep the evidence tied to the page it came from. A folder of copied paragraphs is harder to compare and revisit than a small, traceable source ledger.

Start with a question and a scope

Write one sentence describing what you are trying to find out. Add boundaries such as date range, location, population, product version, or definition. This gives you a reason to keep or discard a source and makes it easier to notice when two pages are not answering the same question.

Make one note per source

Record the title, author or organization, publication or update date, original URL, and date you viewed it. Then capture a short summary in your own words, a relevant quotation or data point, its heading or location, and any limitations. Separate a direct quotation from your interpretation so a later reader can tell which is which.

Do not copy more text than you need or share material you do not have permission to distribute. Where a quotation is useful, keep it limited, accurate, and attributed. Add a link to the source rather than relying on a copied excerpt to stand alone.

Separate observations from synthesis

Keep the source notes separate while collecting evidence. Then make a synthesis note that groups the claims relevant to your question and points back to source IDs. Mark whether a statement is supported by one source, repeated by independent sources, disputed, or still unknown. Repeated wording across sites may come from the same original announcement, so repetition alone is not independent confirmation.

Use a retrieval system you will maintain

Use the simplest structure that helps you find the notes again: a folder by project, a dated notebook, a spreadsheet index, or tags in a notes app. Choose predictable filenames such as a short topic plus date or source ID. Keep an index with the research question and links to the individual records. Avoid building a complex taxonomy before you know which distinctions you need to retrieve.

For a multi-page question, keep each webpage as its own source and ask the AI to compare only after the notes have clear labels. The multi-webpage guide covers that process. This article is about organizing ongoing research notes; for preparing one scholarly paper as AI input, see the research-article guide.

Review notes before reuse

  • Can you open the saved link and identify the exact source later?
  • Did you preserve the date, version, units, or population needed to interpret the evidence?
  • Is the note clear about what the source states versus what you inferred?
  • Are conflicting or missing details visible rather than silently resolved?
  • Are permissions and sensitive details handled before sharing the note with an AI tool?

If you use Markdown, headings make the record scannable and links can keep the title paired with its destination. Learn how to retain source links and how to edit Markdown notes.
