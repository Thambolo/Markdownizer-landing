Research workflow

How do I ask AI questions about multiple webpages?

Keep each page's identity and claims separate first, then ask a comparison question that allows for missing or conflicting evidence.

To ask an AI about multiple webpages, make sure the tool can access each page or provide authorized excerpts, label every source, and ask a question that compares specific facts rather than blending the pages into one account. Then check the answer against each original. A list of URLs alone does not show that the tool opened every page.

Confirm access one source at a time

Web search or browsing features vary by app, account, model, and workspace settings. If the tool has web access, ask it to report which pages it could and could not reach before comparing them. OpenAI's ChatGPT Search documentation explains that available search responses may include citations and that users should review sources. Do not infer that a search citation represents every URL you supplied.

If a page is behind a login or unavailable, do not ask the AI to work around the restriction. Use another source you are authorized to access, or provide only an excerpt you are allowed to share. Keep the reason a source is missing visible in the final comparison.

Make a source register

Before comparing, record a short source ID, title, publisher, URL, and publication or update date. Add version, product, location, or population details when they affect what the page means. A source label gives the AI and the reader a stable way to refer back to the evidence.

Source A - Agency guidance, updated 2026-08-10
URL: https://example.org/guide
Relevant section: Eligibility, paragraphs 2-4
Excerpt: [reviewed text you are permitted to share]

Source B - Provider documentation, version 4
URL: https://example.net/docs
Relevant section: Account limits
Excerpt: [reviewed text you are permitted to share]

The example sources and dates above are placeholders, not real evidence. In your own notes, keep one source block per page and do not remove headings or qualifications that identify the claim's scope.

Ask for a staged comparison

  1. Ask for a brief evidence note from each source independently: what it says, what it does not say, and where the statement appears.
  2. Ask the tool to compare only specified fields, such as effective date, covered group, requirement, exception, and evidence location.
  3. Tell it to report disagreement, ambiguity, and missing information instead of choosing a winner automatically.
  4. Ask for citations or section labels tied to each row, then verify that each source supports its attributed claim.

This approach reduces accidental source mixing. It does not establish that the sources are authoritative, current, independent, or complete. Those are review questions for you.

Watch for differences that look like contradictions

Two pages may refer to different dates, versions, populations, definitions, locations, or product tiers. A newer FAQ and an older policy may be describing separate situations. Record the difference before deciding that one source is wrong. If the pages make genuinely incompatible claims, quote both fairly and say what additional evidence would resolve the conflict.

For webpage capture, Markdownizer can convert the active page you can view into text with headings and links, but it does not fetch a group of URLs for the AI or verify their claims. Check each converted page yourself and preserve its source information. For a broader method, see how to compare conflicting web sources and how to keep source links in notes.
