Copy cleanup

How do I remove menus and ads from copied webpage text?

Clean the copied result in an editor, removing what is clearly unrelated while preserving context that explains the page's claims.

To remove menus and ads from copied webpage text, paste the material into an editor, identify repeated or unrelated sections, and delete them only after checking that they are not part of the source evidence. Keep the page title, author or publisher, date, link, captions, warnings, and any navigation needed to find a cited section. Review the finished text against the original page before sharing it.

Start from the source's purpose

Decide what you need from the page: its article, a set of instructions, a policy clause, a product specification, or a table. That scope helps you recognize likely clutter, but do not treat every repeated or promotional block as irrelevant. An alert, disclosure, update note, footnote, or author bio may change how the main text should be interpreted.

Use a careful cleanup pass

  1. Save the unedited copy or a note of the original URL before making changes.
  2. Remove repeated site navigation, cookie notices, unrelated recommendations, and obvious ads when they do not affect the task.
  3. Check repeated headings and links; a repeated block might be a sidebar, but it might also be a source citation or an important warning.
  4. Keep captions with figures, table headers with values, and footnotes with the claims they qualify.
  5. Mark any text you could not interpret instead of deleting it silently.
  6. Read the cleaned text once more while looking at the original page.

Search for duplicated menu labels, repeated calls to action, and unexplained fragments. In a long paste, a clear heading may help you find the article boundary. Avoid global replace rules that remove every occurrence of a word such as "subscribe" or "note"; the same word may appear in the article itself.

Choose the capture scope separately from cleanup

A page-capture tool may offer a focused main-content mode and a wider page-body mode. A narrower mode can reduce some surrounding page material, while a wider mode can include more navigation and repeated content. Neither automatically removes every ad or identifies which sections matter to your question; dynamic or lazy-loaded content can also be absent. Review the output either way.

Markdownizer's existing guide to Smart selection vs. full-page capture explains capture scope. This article is about the next step: editing and checking the copied result. Markdownizer does not promise ad blocking, universal cleanup, or automatic redaction.

Keep enough context to reuse the excerpt

Retain the title, source URL, relevant section heading, date or version, and short explanation of what you removed if that could affect interpretation. If you use the excerpt for research, cite the source and keep a separate note of uncertainty. If you paste it into an AI service, confirm that you have permission to share it and review private link parameters as well as visible text.

For the reasons copied text may look different from a page, read why copied webpage text can look messy. For a review of sensitive content before sharing, see the sharing checklist.

A quick final check

  • Does the excerpt still contain the evidence needed for the task?
  • Did cleanup remove an exception, date, caption, or source label?
  • Are links still attached to descriptive text and checked for private data?
  • Is the cleaned content permitted to be shared with the intended destination?
