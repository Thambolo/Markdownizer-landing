What Actually Saves Tokens: Changing Format or Removing Irrelevant Content?

Separate two different changes: how the same content is represented and how much source material you include.

If you want to reduce prompt tokens, first ask whether every part of the source helps answer the question. Removing irrelevant navigation, repeated boilerplate, or unrelated sections can change the amount of information you send. Changing HTML into Markdown or plain text changes its representation, but the token difference for a particular sample may be smaller, larger, or absent. Measure those two changes separately rather than promising that one format always saves tokens.

Do not trim away the material that makes a claim interpretable: a definition, unit, source date, method, exception, or limitation. Fewer tokens are not a goal by themselves, and a smaller prompt does not guarantee a better AI response. For source scope, start with how much material to include.

Separate scope reduction from format conversion

Suppose a webpage has a useful article surrounded by menus and recommendations. A conversion step might remove some page structure, select a main-content area, or express the result in Markdown. Those operations are not the same:

  • Scope reduction removes content, such as repeated navigation or a section unrelated to the task.
  • Format conversion changes how the retained content is represented, such as Markdown headings instead of HTML tags.
  • Summarization replaces source passages with a shorter restatement and may omit detail or introduce interpretation.

If you compare the original HTML with a shorter Markdown result, you cannot tell whether a token difference came from tags, omitted content, changed whitespace, or rewritten wording. To test format alone, keep the text constant. To test scope, fix the format and remove only a clearly identified section.

Trim in a way you can explain

  1. Write the question and the facts needed to answer it.
  2. Keep the relevant source headings, definitions, units, dates, methods, exceptions, and limitations.
  3. Remove repeated menus, unrelated product descriptions, and duplicate statements only after checking that they are not needed.
  4. Keep a source title, URL, and version or checked date outside the shortened excerpt.
  5. Compare the final text with the original and note what you omitted.

A product comparison might need specifications, variants, currency, billing period, and a date, while leaving out a site's general navigation. A research question about a finding may need the methods and limitations, so keeping only the abstract could be a misleading shortcut. A code question may need the exact prerequisites and command warning, even if they add length.

Count the complete prompt, not just the source

Your input can also include the instruction, conversation history, tool-provided context, files, or images. A count of one pasted excerpt is not necessarily the total charged or accepted by the destination. Limits and accounting can vary by model and interface. Markdownizer shows a character count, not a model token count. Check the receiving tool's current limits and use a matching tokenizer when one is provided.

The measurement article compares invented text samples with the public OpenAI Tokenizer and records its model-family selector. Those counts are examples for those exact inputs only; they do not establish typical savings from Markdown, CSV, or page extraction.

Keep readability and auditability in the budget

A little structure can make a prompt easier for a person to inspect and can keep values beside their labels. For plain-text conversions, headings and lists may be useful even if they add markers. Conversely, a source full of repeated details can obscure the material that matters. The balance depends on your task, not an abstract minimum token target.

After trimming, ask yourself whether you can still answer "where did this fact come from?" and "what qualification applies to it?" If not, restore context or split the task into smaller, traceable questions. For a deeper comparison of common representations, read Markdown vs. HTML, Markdown vs. plain text, or table formats.

Respect sharing rules while reducing scope

Only share sources you are allowed to use with the destination service. Removing a name from the excerpt does not automatically remove identifiers from links, attributes, or other context. See the pre-sharing checklist. Markdownizer's hybrid processing model is described in its privacy policy; converting a page and later pasting it into AI are separate steps.
