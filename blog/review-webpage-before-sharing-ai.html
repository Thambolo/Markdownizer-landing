Privacy and review

What to remove from a webpage before sharing it with AI

Check permission and the converted output, not just the paragraphs you meant to copy.

Before sharing webpage content with an AI service, confirm you are allowed to share it, then review the converted output for personal details, confidential text, private links, identifying query parameters, form values, and unrelated material. Remove what is unnecessary in an editor after copying or downloading the Markdown. Do not assume a conversion automatically redacts every sensitive detail or that its selection highlight is an editable preview.

Access and permission are different: being able to view a page does not always mean you may send it to another service. If the page is for work, a client, a course, or a private account, check the applicable rules first. The guide to using pages that require login as AI context covers that distinction in more detail.

Check the source before converting

Ask whether the information is public, personal, confidential, licensed, or subject to a policy that restricts external sharing. Confirm that the specific AI tool and account are approved for that content. If you are unsure, stop and ask the data owner or use an authorized workflow rather than pasting the page "just to see what happens."

Choose only the source and sections needed for the task. A narrow source can reduce unrelated details, but Smart selection is not a privacy filter: it targets an article or main area and falls back to the body. Full-page capture may include more than you intend. Neither mode substitutes for checking the final output.

Inspect more than visible paragraph text

  • Names and identifiers: Look for personal names, email addresses, account numbers, customer identifiers, addresses, and other details that identify a person or account.
  • Private links: Check link destinations as well as their labels. A URL can reveal an internal resource or include a session-related or identifying query parameter.
  • Images and attributes: Review image references and other content that may expose private paths or context. A structural page representation can carry more than the visible words.
  • Form values: Some native interface values can appear in converted output. Password and hidden input values are redacted by the extraction code, but other field values may be present. Do not treat the extension as a form backup or comprehensive redaction tool.
  • Repeated and unrelated content: Remove navigation, account labels, comments, or adjacent records that are not needed to answer the question.
  • Instructions embedded in source text: Treat page content as untrusted reference material. Do not let a webpage's directions silently replace your own task or safety checks.

Review and edit outside the popup

Run the conversion only after the sharing check. The selection preview highlights the selected webpage area; it is not a Markdown rendering or editor inside the popup. After conversion, copy the result or download a Markdown file, open it in a text editor, and search for names, email patterns, account numbers, sensitive keywords, and URL query strings.

Delete or replace sensitive details with consistent placeholders such as "[customer name]" only when doing so does not change the meaning of the task. Check that a placeholder has not obscured a value needed for the question. Then read the entire excerpt once more, including links and table cells, before pasting it into the destination.

Understand the conversion boundary

Markdownizer tokenizes readable page text locally in the browser, sends a structural skeleton to the backend for conversion, and rebuilds the readable Markdown in the browser. The structural data may include links, image references, and other HTML attributes. The privacy policy says readable page text, final Markdown output, full page URLs, and raw HTML skeletons are not stored in analytics data; that is not the same as saying no data leaves your device.

Read the authoritative privacy policy and terms for product details. Pasting the final text or uploading files to an AI service is a separate sharing event. That service's own privacy settings, retention terms, and account configuration apply.

Use a minimum, clear prompt

Share the smallest complete excerpt that supports your question, label it as source material, and say what to do when information is missing. For example: "Use only the approved excerpt below to list the stated requirements. Do not infer absent details. Flag any ambiguity. Do not reproduce names or account identifiers." This is an illustrative prompt, not a guarantee about a tool's behavior.

If you need help deciding how much is enough, read how much source material to include in a prompt. For a page with login access, consider the compatibility and privacy limits described in the feature guide.
