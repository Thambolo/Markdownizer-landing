Verification

How do I check whether an AI summary is accurate?

Treat the summary as a set of claims to verify, not as a substitute for the source.

Check an AI summary by comparing each important claim with the original source, verifying numbers and quotations exactly, and restoring any caveats or uncertainty the summary omitted. Use the source itself rather than asking the same AI to confirm its answer. A fluent summary can still misstate scope, blend separate claims, or leave out a qualification.

Turn the summary into checkable claims

Break a paragraph into individual statements. Mark each as directly stated, paraphrased, inferred, not stated, or contradicted by the source. For each important statement, record the source heading, page, table, or a short quotation that would let another person find the evidence.

Summary claim: [one statement at a time]
Source location: [heading, page, table, or paragraph]
Evidence: [short quotation or exact value]
Status: stated / partial / inferred / not stated / contradicted
Missing caveat or scope:
Follow-up check:

This ledger is a review aid, not an accuracy score. Use it to make the verification trail visible, especially for decisions where a single exception changes the result.

Check the details that are easy to distort

  • Numbers: Check the value, unit, denominator, date range, and whether it is an estimate or measured result.
  • Scope: Confirm the population, region, version, sample, or conditions match the source.
  • Qualifications: Look for exceptions, uncertainty, limitations, and counterexamples near the original claim.
  • Attribution: Check who said or concluded something; do not turn a quoted opinion into an established fact.
  • Quotes and citations: Verify the wording and ensure the linked source supports the exact statement.
  • Completeness: Check whether a nearby section changes the interpretation or states a different result.

Check for omissions as well as errors

A summary can contain only true statements and still be misleading if it omits the context that matters. Ask what the source says about limitations, methods, exceptions, and what it does not cover. For a research report, keep the study design and limitations close to the findings; for technical documentation, check version and prerequisites; for policies, retain effective dates and eligibility conditions.

OpenAI's help guidance on whether ChatGPT tells the truth advises users to verify important information and check external references. The principle applies to AI summaries generally: use the original or an authoritative source for the task at hand, not confidence or citation appearance as a substitute for checking.

Handle sources and citations carefully

If an AI response provides citations, open each one. Confirm the title and URL, locate the supporting passage, and check the page's date. Search citations can lead to incomplete, outdated, or unrelated material. A source list is useful only when the evidence actually supports the associated claim.

If you supplied the text yourself, keep source identity and section boundaries with it. If the assistant was asked to summarize a URL, first confirm the page was actually accessed. See how to request source citations and why a pasted link may not have been read.

When the answer needs extra scrutiny

For medical, legal, financial, safety, employment, or other consequential decisions, consult the applicable authoritative source or a qualified professional. Ask a second person to review the source trail when appropriate. Do not use a summary to make an important determination if you cannot verify the underlying evidence.

If a summary is wrong, correct it using the original text and preserve the correction in your notes. For a better initial request, see how to summarize a long article without missing key details.
